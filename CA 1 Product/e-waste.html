What is E-Waste?

E-waste is discarded electrical and electronic equipment and components — anything powered by electricity or a battery that is no longer used or functional.

In simple terms

When a laptop stops working, a charger goes unused, or you throw away an old phone — that becomes e-waste. It includes both the device itself and all its parts: circuit boards, screens, cables, and batteries.

Common examples of e-waste

Smartphones

Phones contain precious metals like gold, silver and copper, plus hazardous materials like lithium batteries and lead.

Laptops

Laptops contain lithium-ion batteries, rare-earth magnets and circuit boards with recoverable components.

Computers

Desktop computers contribute CPUs, RAM, motherboards and drives — rich in recoverable metals.

Monitors

Older monitors contain lead in glass and mercury in backlights; modern ones include sensitive displays.

Printers

Printers contain plastic, metal, ink cartridges and electronic boards that should be recycled separately.

Televisions

TVs contain hazardous mercury, lead and flame retardants as well as valuable glass and metals.

Chargers

Chargers and adapters are small but numerous — their cables and circuits add to e-waste volumes.

Batteries

Batteries are hazardous e-waste. They must never go in normal trash due to fire and chemical risks.

Components

Circuit boards, chips and other electronic components collected from devices and manufacturing are also e-waste.

Why e-waste is different from normal household waste

Contains hazardous materials — lead, mercury, cadmium, brominated flame retardants that are dangerous to health and soil.

Contains valuable resources — gold, silver, copper, palladium, rare earths that are lost when simply thrown away.

Holds personal data — devices store data that must be securely wiped before disposal.

Often still repairable — unlike common trash, e-waste frequently has recovery and reuse value.
